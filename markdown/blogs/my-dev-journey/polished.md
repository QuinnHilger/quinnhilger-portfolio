# My Journey Into Software Engineering

I've been reflecting lately on how I arrived at where I am today. It's been quite a journey.

## The Beginning

I first started coding in high school through an introductory Java course. I distinctly remember being completely bewildered by the concept of a "for loop." But eventually, something clicked.

The moment I got my first program to work—a simple calculator—I felt like I had unlocked a superpower. I could instruct the computer to do things! That sense of wonder has never really faded.

## College Years

UCLA was intense. The Computer Science program pushed me to my limits. There were moments when I genuinely considered giving up—some of those algorithms courses were particularly challenging.

However, I also encountered incredible people and contributed to fascinating projects. My fraternity experience helped me develop interpersonal skills that I didn't initially recognize would prove essential in my professional life.

### Key Moments

- Working until 4 AM to complete my first substantial project
- Securing my first internship at Pacific Defense
- Discovering that production code at companies is often messier than academic projects
- Realizing that communication skills are just as crucial as technical abilities

## What I've Learned

In retrospect, here are my most significant takeaways:

1. **Imposter syndrome is universal** — Everyone experiences it, including senior engineers with decades of experience.
2. **Nobody knows everything** — That's simply impossible, and that's perfectly acceptable.
3. **Ask questions early** — It saves considerable time and prevents unnecessary frustration.
4. **Side projects are invaluable** — SpotMe taught me more practical skills than any classroom could.

## What's Next

I'm energized about what lies ahead. Artificial intelligence is transforming our field, and I'm determined to contribute to that evolution. Working at DraftKings has provided excellent enterprise experience, but I'm continuously seeking new challenges.

If you're reading this and just beginning your journey—persist. It does get easier. Well, perhaps "different" is more accurate, but infinitely more rewarding.

# My Relationship with AI

## Context - My Situation

I graduated from college 8 months ago with a degree in computer science. Then a month later I started my first job as a Software Engineer. This is a weird time to start working as a software engineer. I’ll preface by saying I don’t think anyone knows exactly how their college degree will translate to work. Or what the career they’re pursuing really looks like. Even understanding the day to day of a job prior to living it is difficult. A degree into computer science is probably one of the easiest to translate to career. But the career of being a software engineer 4 years ago is far from the reality of being a software engineer today. Even crazier is who knows what being a software engineer will be like in the future. The workflows, who will be successful and in demand, what job titles look like, what kind of skills will be the most valuable, and other key aspects of work are nearly impossible to predict 1 year into the future. Then trying to get some accurate estimation of those details 5, 10 years from now is hopeless. Maybe this has always been true in software engineering as technology has grown so fast, but the significant step change that occurred while I was in college seems to be unprecedented.

## AI in College

It was the end of my first semester (quarter) of my sophomore year when ChatGPT came out. By the time I got back from winter break, it was clear the future of knowledge work is on a new trajectory. At first the applications of it were small. I’d utilize it for answering trivial questions, slowly replacing google search, performing grunt translations of thoughts to text or code. Then as time went on, referencing ChatGPT felt more useful than exploring stack overflow. Then brainstorming and planning was shifted to a chat interface rather than a messy google doc. As I grew an understanding of how to utilize an LLM, and in parallel the LLM got better at producing a useful probability distribution of the next token in a sequence, the presence of AI was solidified as a partner for all knowledge work.

I’ll also take this time to give myself a little bit of credit. I was pretty pessimistic of the impact of AI on my learning experiences. On one hand, I hated the idea of cheating, so when projects and homeworks were explicitly AI free, I would adhere and produce my work independently. But even when working on projects outside of school work, I’d certainly prefer to act without too much AI presence. While developing in React Native for the SpotMe workout app, I mostly used AI as a syntax dictionary. I knew the system I wanted to implement and how to organize all of the components. But if I’m filling in some CSS or forgot certain method header syntax, AI could fill those in pretty easily. However, I absolutely refused to copy and paste a whole file, component, or non-trivial function into my codebase. That kind of work felt beneath me. I wanted to learn, and had confidence in myself for producing a better product. I felt like I had a chip on my shoulder for producing my own work, coming up with my own ideas, problem solving and implementing by myself. I’d work on school projects with a team and see a classmate open up a PR with lines of code we’ve all seen before…

## New Perspectives

The expectation and incentive structure certainly changes once you leave school. Although there is still an importance in growing and learning. Selfishly growing and learning are very important to position yourself as a valuable employee and set yourself up for the future. But also for the company they benefit from leadership and seniors growing into their role within the org. With that being said, the driving force working in the “real-world” is efficiency and quality of work. The faster you can produce a better output, the better it is for the company. This incentive structure definitely leans into the utilization of tooling assistants much more than school. I’m also very fortunate my employer is encouraging towards adopting AI into daily workflows. We have near unlimited access to all of the newest tools and models (that pass some security process). Plenty of other engineers across the org share their own experiences, tips and tricks, projects they made. It’s honestly a wonderful environment to learn how to utilize AI and grow as a problem solver.

I’m also lucky enough this is all starting to speed up just as I start my software engineering career. AI seems to be shifting from a tool to a paradigm. When you ask people outside of software engineering if they use AI in their work, their answers seem to revolve around whether they use Google or ChatGPT. I’ll hear some people talk about the life changing AI they utilize, which rewrite their emails in a friendlier tone. This is not the world of AI I’m referencing. The AI tools in software development are far beyond the syntax dictionaries or intelligent auto-fill. AI Researcher and software developer Andrej Karpathy calls the LLM capacities of Claude and Codex around December of 2025 were a phase shift in software engineering. This next step in the capabilities of AI, the fact that software engineering is seemingly able to utilize this growth greater than any other field, my employer encouraging and supplying advanced AI tool use all seem to be setting myself up for success. I feel like I was just given all the tools to build myself a successful and impactful career. I could set myself up to give meaning to my life yet still provide for my family. The only problem… These tools didn’t come with an instruction booklet.

## My Attempts

Having no instructions is totally fine though. All of the Amazon and Wayfair furniture sitting in my apartment were very instruction optional. Find a hole and a screw that fits and keep putting pieces together. This worked pretty well and most of the furniture would still survive a 4.0 magnitude earthquake. This isn’t far off from how I’ve attempted to take on this age of AI. Starting with trying to stay up to date with what’s going on. Starting with reading my daily tech newsletter TLDR, to picking up some Software related podcasts like “The Pragmatic Engineer” or “The AI Daily Brief” I’m trying to stay informed. Sharing ideas and participating in AI related discussions at the office certainly help. I’m ingesting all of these thoughts and ideas, letting them fester while going on a run or walking to work. All with the hopes of improving my utilization of these tools.

At work, my main tool I’ve been using for the last few months has been the CLI coding assistant Claude Code. Between this and Codex it’s clearly a differentiated product to the alternatives. But picking a tool is just the start, then you have to figure out how to use it. How do you learn how to best use these tools? My attempt at an answer is to just use it! Along those notes, over the past two months I’ve reached #1 on the Claude Code usage leaderboard for my vertical (~80 engineers). I’m hoping this usage is viewed positively as an ambitious and curious action, rather than being too costly. Also “just using it” is definitely over simplifying the process. Just using it without the right intent or analysis is definitely stepping down the wrong path. It really needs to be approached with an open mind yet critical and preference oriented. I need to ask the right questions and come up with my own answers.

In which contexts should I lean into the AI tools? Currently they’re the most helpful for documentation aggregations, code review, code implementation, and codebase related incident investigations. What configurations should I use? I like to have both user level and repo level claude.md files to define rules, add context, set coding preferences etc. I like to iterate on these rather than attempting to produce a perfect doc on my first try. What tools should I give the agent? I honestly think MCPs are extremely overrated, rarely implemented well, clog up context windows, slow down response time and incorporate bad, uncontrolled context. I do like setting up skills and agents that can reference local markdown files (that I have Claude create) and allowing these to be invoked during use. How do I harness the agent? I like the idea of isolating claude on a dangerously skip perms in some virtual container, however I haven’t executed that yet. Currently I just iterate on allowed permissions (mostly all read perms and in repo file edit access). I’ll also include which commands to run in config files to prevent it from asking for tools that require my permission.

## The Hopeful Future

Wow, looks like I’ve figured it out, easy and done. Right??? Obviously not. Not only are these current answers far from some optimized set of environment controls, but also the optimized set will be constantly changing. Model capabilities, access to tools, and even the demands of a software engineer are set to change so much. So on top of finding my own preferences for utilizing AI, I also need to be open minded and ready to pivot. This does feel scary and sounds like a burden. But the opportunities that these tools have opened up are so exciting. What I can create and accomplish as an engineer is far greater than I could’ve ever imagined before. It’s a super exciting time to be a software engineer. I entered into this field because it felt like you could build so much so easily. You don’t need thousands of study patients and decades of research to produce something amazing. You don’t need large metal cutting machines and printers to bring your ideas to life. Now AI has made what you can accomplish 5, 20, ??? times greater than what was previously possible. This is truly an amazing technology that will change the world and I’m so excited to be engulfed in that world.

Thanks for reading all of that yapping. Whether you’re in the software field or not, I hope this was insightful and inspirational for thinking about and using AI. There is so much that can be talked or thought about. And if you want a happy ending, feel free to stop here. It’s not all sunshine and rainbows.

## The Scary Future

I’ll keep this part relatively short since I ’m not a doomsday expert or dystopian novel author. There are certainly scary futures out there in regards to AI. Machines enslaving humans for energy as we live out our fantasies on pods filled with goo, is not the future I’m talking about. AI is going to be a serious disrupter to what it means to work across the world. Yes there have been significant technological shifts in the past that disrupted what work looks like. It’s pretty obvious if you’re reading this on a computer or phone and not out picking wild blueberries or hunting mammoths. This shift feels a little different though (I’m 23 years old so I experienced a whole lot of technological shifts lol). Selfishly, I think a lot about what software engineering is going to look like. How many software engineers will we have? What will their purpose be? My beliefs are we’ll have relatively solid job growth as a field. Maybe each engineering team will have less developers on it than currently. But I believe software engineers can take over any other knowledge work role. If AI is good enough to reduce the need for software engineers because it can write code so easily, then any smart engineer equipped with that AI can construct agents, models, automations, whatever you want to call it, to make any other career in knowledge work obsolete.

The impact will certainly be much greater than a single career. All of the work currently done by humans will be automated and optimized to be done by fewer and fewer humans. Hopefully we can come up with new stuff to work on just as fast. But that’s certainly a hope. It’s not a guarantee. I do expect there to be some tough times coming up in the future for my generation. Jobs won’t be a guarantee, futures will be harder to predict than ever before. Plans for buying homes or having kids may be delayed. The possibility of tough times for millions of people across the US due to AI reducing jobs seems to be encroaching on the value of 1.

As a species we may become so productive we’ll run out of things we need to do. The feeling of purpose and providing that fuels everyone waking up each day may start to fade. Something that sounds objectively good (productivity) may cause a downfall for society.

But where’s the fun in that perspective? Being a pessimist counting down the days until the end of the world doesn’t sound like an approach to life I’d prefer. So instead I’ll maintain my hope. I believe humans are special little meat sacks who are capable of amazing things. If these amazing things develop into automating away the production of all necessities freeing up humans to satisfy themselves in more grand ways, I’m all for it. Maybe we start singing and dancing more, exploring nature that we’re able to successfully save from climate change due to products of AI. Not sure what the specifics are, but I definitely have hope that we’ll figure it out. Best of luck to the doomsday preppers, I’ll be betting my money on the intellectually advanced apes figuring it out, and thriving in whatever new world comes out of the AI revolution.
